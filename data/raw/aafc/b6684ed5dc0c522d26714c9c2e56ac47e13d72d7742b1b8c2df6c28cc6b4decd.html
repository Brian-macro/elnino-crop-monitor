Canada: Outlook for Principal Field Crops, 2026-09-25

This report updates Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada’s (AAFC) August Outlook for the 2025-26 and 2026-27 crop years, incorporating the latest domestic and international market developments, including trade policies in effect as of September 18, 2026. In Canada, the crop year for most field crops extends from August 1 to July 31, while the crop year for corn and soybeans runs from September 1 to August 31. Global crop markets continue to operate in an environment of elevated uncertainty, driven by ongoing geopolitical tensions, evolving trade policies, and disruptions affecting global transportation and supply chains.

For the 2025-26 crop year, the report provides final estimates by Statistics Canada (STC) for all crops with the exception of corn and soybeans. According to information from STC’s September 9, 2026 report on Stocks of Principal Field Crops as of July 31, 2026, stocks of principal field crops, excluding corn and soybeans, were 47.5% higher than 2025 levels and 37.1% above the previous five-year average. Most crops recorded higher stocks at the end of July, with wheat, excluding durum, posting the largest increase, up 48.8% year over year (y/y), as lower exports coincided with record production. Barley was the only exception, with stocks declining by 24.7%, largely due to robust exports combined with strong domestic demand for animal feed and industrial use. Canola stocks increased 18.9% from 2025, despite brisk exports since December and strong domestic processing from January to July 2026, reflecting large beginning inventories following China’s imposition of tariffs and reduced imports in the first quarter of the crop year. As a result, carry-out stocks (year-end inventories) for all principal field crops are expected to increase significantly, by 42% y/y, and remain 30% above the five-year average. The average price for most field crops are lower y/y, except for corn, canola, soybeans, and mustard seed.

For the 2026-27 crop year, the outlook incorporates preliminary crop production estimates from STC’s recent Model-Based Principal Field Crop Estimates release, based on information available as of August 31, 2026. Production of principal field crops is estimated to decline 5.0% from the record 2025 harvest but remain 9.5% above the previous five-year average. If realized, the 2026 crop would be the second-largest harvest on record. Prairie production is projected to decline 8.3% y/y but remain 8.7% above the previous five-year average. Production in Eastern Canada is projected to increase 8.4% y/y, supported by stronger corn and soybean output. Most other field crops are expected to post lower production result in 2026 as yields return to near average levels. Wheat production is projected to decrease 10.9% y/y, reflecting lower yields and reduced seeded area for both durum and wheat (excluding durum). Oilseed production is expected to increase 1.0% y/y, as a larger soybean crop offsets slightly lower canola production. Coarse grain production is forecast to increase 1.9% y/y, driven by higher corn yields and seeded area, which more than offset declines in other coarse grains. Production of pulses and special crops is projected to fall sharply by 20.9%, as lower yields combine with reduced acreage, particularly for dry peas and lentils. Harvest progress in Western Canada is significantly behind both last year’s pace and the five-year average, as widespread rainfall has delayed fieldwork. The impact of harvest delays and weather conditions on crop quality will become clearer through subsequent releases of the Canadian Grain Commission’s Harvest Sample Program. Carry-out stocks are projected to decrease, reflecting lower production and a return to more normal supply levels.

The next AAFC Outlook for Principal Field Crops is scheduled for release on October 20, 2026. The next major STC release will be survey-based estimates of field crop production on December 4, 2026.

All Wheat

Durum

For 2025-26, Canadian durum supply rose 11% to 7.8 million tonnes (Mt), according to Statistics Canada (STC), the second-largest harvest since the 2016-17 marketing year. In its latest stocks report, STC revised both production (+2%) and carry-in stocks (+3%) upward. Approximately 878 thousand tonnes (kt) of durum were consumed domestically, with feed use increasing 56% year over year due to mixed crop quality. Closing stocks were reported at a comfortable 1.1 Mt, double the previous year's level.

Nearly 75% of Canada's durum supply was exported to international markets. Exports reached a record 5.85 Mt, surpassing the previous year's high due to increased shipments to Turkey (+167 Kt), Italy (+96 Kt), Venezuela (+65 Kt), China (+48 Kt), Mexico (+37 Kt), and Nigeria (+33 Kt). Exports to North Africa, an important market for Canadian durum, declined, particularly to Morocco (-18%), as larger regional harvests reduced import demand.

Internationally, the International Grains Council (IGC), in its latest market report, left global durum supply and demand estimates unchanged, with production at 45.7 Mt, consumption at 37.2 Mt, trade at 9.6 Mt, and ending stocks at 8.5 Mt, up 13% from the previous year. In 2025-26, Canada remained the world's largest durum exporter, accounting for 62% of global trade. On the demand side, the European Union was the largest importer, representing 28% of total imports, followed by Algeria (19%) and the United States (17%).

The final average spot price for Canadian Western Amber Durum, No. 1, 13% protein (CWAD, 1, 13%), in Saskatchewan for 2025-26 was $282/tonne, peaking at $317/tonne at the end of the marketing year.

For 2026-27, STC's model-based estimates project Canadian durum production to decline 12% to 6.4 Mt due to reduced acreage and lower yields. Total supply is forecast at 7.5 Mt, down 4% from 2025-26, although larger-than-average carry-in stocks will help offset the production decline. At the time of writing, harvest progress remains slower than usual due to widespread rainfall. In Saskatchewan, 46% of the durum crop had been harvested as of the first week of September, compared to 65% at the same time last year. Early quality assessments indicate that 23% of the crop was graded CWAD No. 1, below the 10-year average, while 28% was rated CWAD No. 2 and 25% CWAD No. 3. However, further downgrades remain possible with a significant portion of the crop still in the field. In Alberta, the durum harvest was 39% complete, compared to 57% last year, although quality appears more favourable, with 72% of the crop rated CWAD No. 1.

Domestic use is forecast at 1.0 Mt, up 1% year over year, supported by an expected increase in feed and residual use, pending final quality and grade results. Exports, meanwhile, are projected to decline 14% due to reduced shipments to North Africa following improved local harvests. The quality of the Canadian crop will be a key factor influencing export demand, particularly as interest in high-protein durum for blending remains strong. According to the Canadian Grain Commission (CGC), 410 Kt of durum were exported through the licensed elevator system during the first month of the 2026-27 marketing year, compared to just 150 Kt during the same period last year, highlighting strong early-season demand for Canadian durum. However, most shipments to date are likely carryover stocks from the previous crop year, making the current export outlook subject to revision as more of the 2026 crop enters the handling system and its quality profile becomes clearer. Closing stocks are forecast at 1.5 Mt, up 36% from 2025-26.

According to the International Grains Council (IGC), global durum production is projected to increase for the third consecutive year to 39 Mt, up 1% from the previous season. Supported by higher carry-in stocks, total supply is forecast at 48 Mt, an increase of 4% year over year. Global use is expected to rise nearly 3%, driven primarily by increased food consumption. While trade is projected to decline 11% from the previous season, demand for high-protein durum is expected to remain firm amid quality concerns in several key importing countries. Global ending stocks are forecast at 9.9 Mt, up 11% from 2025-26.

The average Saskatchewan producer spot price forecast for CWAD No. 1, 13% protein, in 2026-27 is raised to $280/tonne, supported by expectations for stronger demand for higher-protein durum and deferred prices through February 2027.

Wheat (excluding durum)

For 2025-26, Canadian wheat supplies are estimated at 37.2 Mt following STC’s upward revisions to both production and carry-in stocks. STC increased 2025-26 wheat production by 1% to 33.3 Mt and raised carry-in stocks by 3% to 3.7 Mt. The largest upward revision was made to Canadian Western Red Spring (CWRS) wheat, with production increasing 2% to 24.8 Mt. Domestic use reached 7.7 Mt, up 8% from the previous year, driven by a 16% increase in feed use. Ending stocks are estimated at 5.5 Mt, a 49% increase year over year.

Canada exported 23.9 Mt of wheat to more than 60 countries worldwide in 2025-26, up 2% from 2024-25 and setting a new record high. Export growth was supported by strong demand from key Asian markets, particularly Indonesia, China, and Bangladesh. Canada's largest export destinations by share of total exports were Indonesia (11%), China (10%), Japan (9%), Bangladesh (6%), the United States (6%), and Peru (6%). Globally, Canada ranked as the third-largest wheat exporter in 2025-26, behind Russia and the European Union, accounting for 11% of world wheat exports.

Global wheat production in 2025-26 is estimated at 844 Mt according to the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA), up 5% from the previous year. Total supplies are forecast at 1,105 Mt, an increase of 3%. Global wheat trade is estimated at nearly 228 Mt, up 8% year over year, while worldwide consumption is projected to increase by 2%.

The final average spot price for Canadian Western Red Spring Wheat, No. 1, 13.5% (CWAD, 1, 13.5) in Saskatchewan was $269/tonne, peaking at $314/tonne in June.

For 2026-27, STC estimates Canadian wheat production will decline by 11% in 2026-27 due to lower seeded area and yields. However, supported by larger carry-in stocks, total supplies are expected to fall by only 5% and remain 8% above the five-year average. Spring wheat production is forecast at 26.5 Mt, down 11% from the previous year, while winter wheat production is projected to decline 9% to 3.3 Mt.

Harvest progress varies across the country. In Eastern Canada, harvesting is virtually complete, while frequent rainfall has slowed fieldwork across parts of the Prairies, particularly in Saskatchewan and Alberta. At the time of writing, the spring wheat harvest was 17% complete in Saskatchewan and 14% in Alberta, while Manitoba producers had harvested approximately 70% of the crop. Early quality reports from Alberta indicate that 63% of samples graded in the top class, while Manitoba reports suggest most early-harvested wheat was of good quality, although quality deterioration has been noted in some later-harvested fields. While official Saskatchewan quality data are not yet available, preliminary reports point to mixed results due to highly variable conditions across the province. Domestic use is currently forecast at 7.6 Mt, reflecting expectations for higher feed and residual use, pending a clearer assessment of CWRS quality.

Reduced exportable supplies from the Black Sea region due to ongoing conflict may create additional opportunities for higher-protein spring wheat exports, particularly for blending purposes. According to the CGC, wheat exports through the licensed handling system reached 2.5 Mt in the marketing year to date, up 70% from the same period last year. As with durum, much of this movement likely reflects old-crop carryover stocks, and a clearer picture of export demand will emerge as more new-crop supplies enter the system. For now, the export forecast remains unchanged at 23.4 Mt, down 2% from the previous year, while ending stocks are projected at 4.3 Mt.

In its September World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates (WASDE), the USDA increased its forecasts for global wheat supplies, consumption, and ending stocks, while lowering projected trade. Higher production prospects among major exporters, particularly Australia, lifted global wheat supplies by 3.5 Mt to 1,103 Mt. Global consumption was increased to 826.8 Mt, largely due to stronger feed and residual use. World wheat trade was reduced by 0.9 Mt to 211.8 Mt as lower exports from the Black Sea region more than offset gains elsewhere. Global ending stocks are forecast at 276.3 Mt, up 3.0 Mt from the previous estimate, primarily on larger inventories in Russia, Australia, and Ukraine.

The average producer spot price forecast for Saskatchewan CWRS 1, 13.5% is raised to $310/tonne based on spot prices to date and expectations on Minneapolis markets for the remainder of the crop year.

Coarse grains

Barley

For 2025-26, Canadian barley supplies reached 11.1 million tonnes (Mt), up sharply year over year (y/y), supported by both larger carry-in stocks (which were at an eight-year high) and significantly stronger production. Total supplies are also well above the previous five-year average. Partly due to abundant supplies, both exports and domestic use increased substantially y/y and exceeded the five-year average. The growth was driven primarily by exports, which accounted for the majority of the increase in total disappearance. Despite the larger supply, stronger demand reduced carry-out stocks to below 0.95 Mt, down notably from the previous season but still well above the five-year average.

According to Statistics Canada’s (STC) monthly trade data, exports of raw barley grain during the entire crop year (August – July) totaled 3.5 Mt (+67% versus the previous season; +34% versus the five-year average), with China accounting for over 60% of total shipments, followed by Japan, Saudi Arabia, and the US. Exports of barley product (in grain equivalent) totaled almost 0.7 Mt (-9%; -9%), with the US representing about 60% of total shipments, followed by Japan, Mexico, and South Korea.

The final average Lethbridge feed barley price was $283/tonne (/t), down more than $10/t from 2024-25 and approaching its lowest level in six years.

For 2026-27, Canadian barley production is projected by STC at 9.7 Mt, essentially unchanged from last season, due to yields declining notably from the record highs achieved in the previous season, despite larger area. Provincially, Alberta remains the largest barley-growing region, accounting for 53% of total production in 2026. Saskatchewan follows with 40%, while Manitoba contributes 5%. The remaining production is distributed among the other provinces.

Primarily due to steady production and notably lower carry-in stocks, total supply is projected to decline y/y to reach 10.8 Mt. Despite this decrease, supplies remain well above the five-year average. Total demand is expected to decline y/y, reflecting slower exports. Carry-out stocks are forecast at 1.0 Mt, up more than 5% from the precious season and the five-year average.

The 2026-27 Lethbridge average price is projected at $305/t, up more than $20/t y/y, primarily supported by stronger US corn prices.

Worldwide, the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) projects 2026-27 global barley production at above 160 Mt, up significantly y/y. Among the major barley-exporting countries and regions, production is estimated to increase in the Black Sea region, Australia and Canada. However, these gains are expected to be more than offset by a substantial decline in the EU’s production. Meanwhile, barley production in the rest of the world is estimated to increase sharply, resulting in an overall rise in global output. Trade is anticipated to be subdued in the year, while total consumption strengthens. Ending stocks are forecast at over 23 Mt, higher than the previous season and well above the five-year average.

Corn

For 2025-26, Canadian corn supply is estimated at 18.6 Mt, down modestly y/y, mainly due to significantly lower carry-in stocks and reduced production, despite an expected notable increase in imports. Total domestic use is expected to increase y/y on modest increases in industrial and feed use, while exports are expected to decline sharply. Carry‑out stocks are projected at 2.0 Mt, up sharply from the previous season’s 1.6 Mt but in line with the five-year average.

Imports during the first eleven months of the crop year (September – July) totaled over 1.9 Mt (+21% versus the previous season; -29% versus the five-year average), with the US supplying almost all shipments. Canola

For 2025-26, Statistics Canada (STC) revised their estimates for area, yield, and production. STC marginally raised canola seeded and harvested area, while production was raised from 21.8 million tonnes (Mt) to a new estimated peak of 22.2 Mt. Carry-in stocks were sharply lower year over year (y/y) at 1.6 Mt while imports were also down, falling 17% y/y to 109 thousand tonnes (Kt). However, record output offset the decrease in imports and carry-in stocks, raising total supply of canola by 6% y/y to 23.9 Mt. Compared to the previous five-year average (2020-21 to 2024-25), total supply stands 15% higher than the five-year average.

In STC’s latest data release of supply and disposition of grains in Canada, the agency reports that 2025-26 crush rose 13% y/y to a record 12.89 Mt. This surpasses the previous record of 11.4 Mt crushed in 2024-25 and signifies key changes in the canola industry as new processing capacity has come onstream. According to STC’s latest release of crushing statistics, industry crushed a 2025-26 crop year record of 1.3 Mt in July, up 13% from the previous peak in June. Despite strong demand stemming from the domestic crush sector, Canadian canola exports were still stronger than average. STC reports total exports for 2025-26 reached 8.96 Mt, down 5% from 2024-25 but 12% higher than the previous five-year average. Total carry-out finished the year at 1.90 Mt, rebounding 19% y/y but would still be historically tight (-4% from the average).

The simple average canola price for 2025-26, No.1 Track Vancouver, is finalized at $711/tonne (/t), up 5% from 2024-25 but 12% lower than the five-year average of $811/t.

For 2026-27, canola production is estimated at 22.1 Mt, according to STC’s September 16th preliminary model-based estimates. For major canola producing provinces, lower anticipated production for Manitoba (-5% y/y) and Alberta (-4% y/y) is expected to be offset by higher Saskatchewan prospects (+2% y/y). Growing conditions this season have been variable, ranging from extreme precipitation events to drought across the Prairies. As of the first week of September, Prairie provincial government crop reports remark that canola harvest has begun, albeit slower than average due to regional instances of cool and wet weather. If STC’s national production estimate is realized, this would be the second largest crop on record and 17% above the five-year average of 18.8 Mt. Raised production, a rebound in carry-in stocks, and an average import program brings the Canadian canola supply forecast slightly higher than last year at 24.1 Mt. If realized, 2026-27 total supply would amount to the second highest volume on record following 2019-20’s peak of 24.5 Mt.

On the demand side, total domestic use is projected at a new peak of 14.4 Mt, up 10% from last year and a new record-high on strong anticipated industrial use. Given new processing capacity, profitable crush margins, and strong demand (particularly for Canadian canola oil), the Canadian crush projection has been raised 200 Kt this month to a new peak of 13.9 Mt, surpassing last year’s volume by 8%. Total exports have been lowered this month on account of more canola being used in domestic streams. At 7.7 Mt, this would be a decline of 14% y/y, but still slightly ahead of the five-year average. Total carry-out is projected to rebound 4% from last year to 1.98 Mt, but 2% lower than the five-year average.

The simple average price forecast, No.1 Track Vancouver, has been raised to $775/t, marking a 9% rise from 2025-26.

Key factors to observe are: (i) Prairie weather conditions and the pace of harvest, (ii) the Canadian Grain Commission’s harvest sample program in respect to 2026 canola crop quality, (iii) canola oil and meal demand from major markets, (iv) geopolitical volatilty and its impact on the global oilseed complex in respect to trade flows and prices, and (v) any policy updates pertaining to trade of Canadian canola and canola by-products.

At this time, soybean crush is forecast at 1.75 Mt, stable with last year and virtually on par with the five-year average. Total exports have been lowered to 5.4 Mt this month, which would be 2% above last year’s forecast and 11% above the five-year average. Carry-out is projected to rebound to 534 Kt, up 25% and 21% from last year and the average, respectively. If realized, 2026-27 carry-out would be the second highest level in the past seven years following 2023-24’s 605 Kt.

The simple average soybean price forecast, track Chatham, was raised to $575/t. Although moderately higher than 2025-26, this would be 4% below the five-year average of $598/t.

Globally, total soybean production for 2026-27 was raised marginally by the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) in their latest September supply and demand report. At 442.4 Mt, this puts global soybean production 3% higher than 2025-26. The upwards revision primarily reflects stronger US soybean prospects; the USDA raised their domestic production outlook this month to 123.4 Mt (+6% y/y). With higher expected carry-in, imports, and production, global soybean supply is projected at a record 758.0 Mt, surpassing last year’s previous record of 743.1 Mt. The USDA raised global crush projection as well this month to 442.4 Mt; if realized, this would be 3% higher than last year. Global soybean trade is projected to expanded y/y to 191.6 Mt. With strong global demand and use for soybeans, total carry-out is projected by the USDA to contract to 124 M.0 Mt, down 1% from last year, but still considerably high historically at 10% above the five-year average of 112.4 Mt.

The US average soybean price forecast for 2026-27 was raised this month to US$12/bushel ($US441/t).

Dry Peas

For 2025‑26, exports were sharply higher than the 2024‑25 level at 2.66 million tonnes (Mt) due to increased shipments to Bangladesh and Pakistan. This was partly offset by decreased demand from India. Domestic use was lower compared to the previous year. The average dry pea price was $300/tonne (/t), 26% lower than the 2024‑25 price due to higher global supply and a sharp increase in Canadian carry‑out stocks to record levels.

For 2026‑27, Canadian dry pea production is estimated by Statistics Canada (STC) to fall by 17% from 2025‑26, to 3.26 Mt, largely due to lower yields and decreased area. Saskatchewan and Alberta are expected to account for 53% and 42% of the dry pea production, respectively, with the remainder in Manitoba and British Columbia. As a result, total supply is forecast to be similar to the previous year due to record carry‑in stocks but lower production. Exports are forecast to rise to 2.7 Mt, with India, China, and Bangladesh continuing to be Canada’s top markets. Carry‑out stocks are forecast to fall, but remain burdensome. The average price is expected to be higher than 2025‑26 at $310/t, due to smaller dry pea crops expected in Canada, Russia and the EU.

In the US, area seeded to dry peas for 2026‑27 is forecast by the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) to fall by 3% from 2025‑26, to 1.13 million acres (Mac) (0.46 million hectares (Mha)). This is largely due to a fall in area in North Dakota. With lower abandonment and higher yields, US dry pea production is forecast USDA to rise 13% to 0.95 Mt. The major US export markets for dry peas are expected to continue to be China, Canada, and the Philippines.

Lentils

For 2025‑26, lentil exports rose to 2.42 Mt, up 33% from the previous year. Of this, 1.57 Mt were red lentil types, with 0.85 Mt consisting of the green lentil types. The leading export markets were Turkey, India, and the United Arab Emirates. Total domestic use was lower than the previous year at 0.27 Mt. Carry‑out stocks rose sharply to a record 1.28 Mt. The average Canadian lentil price fell 37% to $500/t. No.1 large green lentil prices maintained a crop year premium of $43/t over No.1 red lentil prices.

For 2026‑27, lentil production is estimated to fall by 0.9 Mt to 2.47 Mt due to lower yields and area seeded. By province, Saskatchewan is expected to account for 83% of the lentil production and 17% in Alberta. With the rise in carry-in stocks, total supply is forecast to decrease by only 4% to 3.83 Mt. Exports are forecast to be similar at 2.4 Mt. Carry‑out stocks are expected to be lower, but remain burdensome at a 1.15 Mt. The average price for all grades is forecast to be marginally higher than 2025‑26, at $520/t, due to lower carry‑out stocks and expectations for a decrease in world production.

In the US, the area seeded to lentils for 2026‑27 is forecast by the USDA at 0.63 Mac (0.25 Mha), 42% lower than 2025‑26, due to decreased plantings in Montana and North Dakota. However, with higher yields and lower abandonment, US lentil production is forecast by USDA at 0.37 Mt, down 23% from last year. The main US export markets for lentils are expected to continue to be Canada, Mexico, India, and the EU, particularly Spain.

Sunflower Seed

For 2025-26, sunflower seed exports were lower at 31 Kt due to decreased demand from the US. Carry‑out stocks rose as total demand decreased more than supply, which declined due to lower domestic use and exports. The total average Canadian price for sunflower seed fell from the previous year due to weaker oilseed- but stronger confectionery-type prices.
